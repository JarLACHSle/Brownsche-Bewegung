# Brownian motion and Perrins study of the atmospheric altitude formula
## A project in the Mathesis lab at TU Berlin


>When Perrin seized upon Brownian movement as a phenomenon for detailed study, many mysteries had been illuminated, though irksome questions still remained. There was in the current colloid literature a renewed interest in the Brownian particles as a thing in themselves, with opinion still divided over the cause of their activity. […] Perrin moved into a new direction for proofs of the kinetic theory as a cause of Brownian movement.
>
> - Nye, Mary Jo (1972) Molecular Reality- A Perspective on the Scientific work of Jean Perrin, London, p. 102

**Goal**: Particle brownian motion is by no means a phenomenon of just two dimensions. Even in Jean Perrin's early investigations, the concentration of Brownian particles at different heights, even within samples that are only fractions of a millimeter high, plays an important role. The “disappearance” of observed particles from the limited depth of field of the microscope poses a major hurdle in these observations, as will be shown. Nevertheless, Perrin and his student Chaudesaigues subsequently succeed in tracking individual particles over periods of up to 25 minutes. In this project, we want to approach these remarkable circumstances and compare them with the perspectives offered by computer simulation. To this end, our main goal is first to program a three-dimensional simulation of Brownian motion of at least one suspended particle and visualize it accordingly. Gravitational force will also be taken into account. Furthermore, using a similar approach to Perrin, we want to try to observe Brownian particles restricted to different height levels and assess how (or whether at all) it is possible to keep a single particle in view for such a long time. Finally, our project work will also focus on investigating concentration (or related variables) as a function of altitude and, in particular, the relationship to or connection with the atmospheric altitude formula.
